# RP2040 heap-fragmentation repair and V9→V10 cutover

Date: 2026-08-17 (America/Los_Angeles)

This record is the reproducibility ledger for the Pololu 3pi+ 2040 failure
investigation. It separates diagnostic evidence, source changes, physical
deployment, and result-lineage decisions. It does not promote diagnostic calls
or failed attempts into the publication result set.

## Observed failure

V9 retained seven technical attempt failures. Six failed while receiving a
`DATA` header chunk with `MemoryError` requests of 381–383 bytes; one failed
inside an ACBBA allocator call requesting 256 bytes. The failures occurred on
multiple boards and were not a bad-worker-1 identity, cable, core-affinity, or
parity problem. There were no fatal worker events or parity failures.

The small requested byte counts do not mean that the board had only a few
hundred physical bytes left. MicroPython needs an available heap block for each
allocation. A fragmented heap can therefore reject a small contiguous request
even when total free memory is much larger.

## Hardware and upstream guidance checked

- Pololu documents the 3pi+ 2040 control board as an RP2040 system with 264 KB
  RAM and 16 MB flash: <https://www.pololu.com/docs/0J86/all>.
- The RP2040 datasheet documents 264 KB of on-chip SRAM split across six banks:
  <https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf>.
- MicroPython documents `gc.mem_free()`, explicit `gc.collect()`, and allocation
  thresholds: <https://micropython.org/resources/docs/en/latest/library/gc.html>.
- MicroPython's constrained-device guidance recommends timely collection to
  limit fragmentation: <https://micropython.org/resources/docs/en/latest/reference/constrained.html>.

The sealed MicroPython v1.24 firmware was retained. No firmware update,
`main.py` change, motor command, or sensor initialization was performed.

## Top-K comparison

`/home/agxorin/topk_filter_study/Log.md` shows that the prior Top-K study was not
free of memory limits: Bayesian DGA cases failed at 1,024-byte allocations and
high-K DGA/DMCHBA cases were unusable. Its successful cases reduced candidate
work sharply (in one recorded example, 50 candidates to 1), used bounded or
preallocated workspaces, and unloaded allocator modules when switching
algorithms. Those changes explain much of its lower wall time and heap demand;
applying its Top-K candidate reduction here would change the allocator problem
and publication estimand.

## Root cause and repair

The current persistent runtime loaded all six collaborative allocators although
one physical mission uses only one. On isolated board B, a clean worker had
223,168 bytes free; importing the worker plus all allocator modules reduced
that to about 93,440 bytes. Loading only the active allocator retained about
114,128 bytes, a gain of roughly 20.7 KB over the prior persistent runtime.

The integrity `CHECK` also read all modules in 4 KiB buffers without collecting
before reporting/continuing. Before repair it reported about 40,992 bytes free;
after an explicit post-check collection it reported about 161,568 bytes free.

The implemented repair is common to every deployed algorithm:

1. lazily import only the selected allocator and unload the prior
   algorithm-specific module between missions;
2. explicitly collect after module-integrity hashing and before timed allocator
   entry (collection remains outside `W_alloc`);
3. allocate header/part transfer buffers once at their declared exact length,
   write decoded chunks into fixed slices, and collect dead chunk temporaries;
4. retain the original 384-byte header chunks after they passed the repaired
   heap path, avoiding extra USB acknowledgement overhead.

No candidate horizon, Top-K filter, algorithm rule, authoritative AGX output,
parity projection, virtual-time rule, or device timer boundary was loosened.

A four-context, 50-task ACBBA diagnostic completed four hardware calls with
strict AGX/RP2040 parity. Per-call allocator time was 16.37–16.49 seconds.
Heap free before each timed call was 103,872–104,000 bytes; the lowest recorded
post-call value was 20,992 bytes. The diagnostic is not a publication result.

passed strict parity. Its minimum pre/post-call heap values were 103,920 and
23,328 bytes; PSETUP transfer took 1.98–2.29 seconds per call. This is why the
production host chunk size was not reduced.

## Result preservation and affected trials

At the 2026-08-17T19:39:37Z cutover, V9 had exactly 26 successful missions,
forming 13 complete Eager/B4 pairs. Their job IDs and immutable completion-file
hashes are sealed in `audit/hardware_v9_completed_26.json`. V10 excludes exactly
those complete blocks and schedules the remaining 70 missions. This prevents
every successfully finished physical trial from being rerun.

Four unpromoted/abandoned V9 attempts are eligible to run cleanly in V10 because
they never became successful results:

- `ACBBA__medium__trace_0000__count_b4`
- `ACBBA__medium__trace_0001__eager_b1`
- `ACBBA__medium__trace_0002__eager_b1`
- `ACBBA__medium__trace_0003__count_b4`

The allocator decisions of the 26 preserved results are not expected to change:
the repair is outside the algorithm and every diagnostic call retained strict
parity. RP2040 timing can nevertheless be affected indirectly. With more heap,
post-fix calls may trigger fewer automatic collections *inside* `W_alloc`, so
V9 timing may be slightly higher or noisier than V10 timing. This applies in
principle to all 26 preserved V9 jobs, especially mature ACBBA states. The
source/build lineage must therefore remain a reported timing stratification;
it must not be hidden by copying V9 directories into V10.
